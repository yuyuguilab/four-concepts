# Four Concepts, Four Types: Levels, Boundaries, and a Translation Protocol for Intelligence, Human Intelligence, Artificial Intelligence, and AGI

*Yugui Yu · YYG Strategy Lab · they4897@gmail.com*

The fourth concept paper of a research program (preceded by the theory paper *Fitting the Practice, Not the Works: A Human-Referential Theory of AGI*, the methodology paper *Fitting the Practice: A Data Methodology for Frontier Model Training*, and the concept-unbundling paper *Two Different Goals: Dissociating the Machine Mind from Artificial Intelligence*).

## In one sentence

The four terms do not sit on one ladder: intelligence asks for measurement; human intelligence asks for norms; artificial intelligence asks for artifact criteria; AGI asks for a reference frame.

## Three claims

1. **Typology**: the four terms are four concept types — a capacity concept (intelligence), a sample concept (human intelligence), an artifact concept (artificial intelligence), and a reference-frame concept (AGI). Each type has a different form of "completed definition"; the popular "narrow–general–super" ladder is a narrative device that strings four concept types on a single line.
2. **Layered structure**: within each concept, distinguish the ontological layer (what it is), the epistemic layer (what evidence supports attributing it), and the methodological layer (what procedure detects it). The chronic root of definitional disputes is three illicit slides: operationalism (measurement passing as definition), the mereological fallacy (mechanism passing as the location of a capacity), and criterion promotion (artifact criteria passing as conceptual content).
3. **Translation protocol**: a table mapping engineering observations (circuits, features, gradients, benchmark scores) to the conceptual claims they license, each row with conditions of validity; plus a catalogue of four illicit forms (locational, part-whole misattribution, promotion, universalization).
